[L0012] Bit-parity has a PHYSICS domain limit — the reference-only 1-ULP perturbation test is the discriminator between "port bug" and "impossible in principle" | candidate | added: 2026-07-23 | tags: parity-oracle, swarm-dynamics | lesson: When one parity scenario fails by ~5 orders of magnitude while its neighbours pass at rms 0, the established causes are protocol mismatch (L0002) or a genuine port bug — but there is a third: the REGIME ITSELF is chaotic, and no implementation could pass. The decisive test costs one render and involves no C++ at all: perturb the JS reference's initial state by 1 ULP and re-render JS-vs-JS. If that self-divergence matches the C++-vs-JS rms and GROWS over the render (positive Lyapunov exponent), sample-exact parity is impossible in principle — Math.sin and std::sin are not identically rounded, and the dynamics amplify the last bit. Bracket first (divergence should scale with the destabilising params — coupling × spread here) so the ULP test confirms rather than excuses. Resolution is never a loosened ε: remove the golden, cover the regime with a behavioural anchor (bounded, NaN-clean, the qualitative claim the param exists to make, checked with margin), and probe the anchor's own regime — a K where coupling lifts R by 0.006 is chaotic jitter that could flip sign on a legitimate refactor; anchor where the effect is unmistakable. [L0014] Measuring a weak residue against a strong signal: sample where the signal ISN'T — summing "everything that isn't the signal" measures window leakage, not the residue | candidate | added: 2026-07-28 | tags: parity-oracle, realtime-perf | lesson: Building the shape lab's aliasing gate, the obvious metric was "sum every FFT bin that is not within a couple of bins of a harmonic, divide by harmonic energy." It reported a textbook-clean polyBLEP sawtooth at -27.7 dB, which is absurd — a correct polyBLEP saw is around -150 dB. The metric was not measuring aliasing at all: with a dozen strong harmonics below Nyquist, each one's Hann-window skirt contributes a little to thousands of "non-harmonic" bins, and the sum of thousands of tiny leakages dominated the actual fold-down energy. Every comparison built on it would have been noise-vs-noise, and — worse — it would have looked plausible, because the RANKING between configs was still roughly right. The fix is to sample the residue where the signal cannot reach: at the inter-harmonic MIDPOINTS ((k+0.5)*f0), the nearest harmonic is hundreds of bins away and Hann leakage there is below -100 dB, so whatever remains is genuinely alias. Corrected, the same test read -149.5 dB for the clean saw and exposed a real 87 dB spread between configurations that the broken metric had compressed into 15 dB. GENERAL RULE: when measuring a small quantity in the presence of a large one, do not define the small quantity as "total minus large" — find a place in the domain where the large quantity is structurally absent and measure there. Applies to spectral residue, noise floors, crosstalk, and any A-minus-B difference metric where B is orders of magnitude bigger. | evidence: docs/design/shape-lab.html aliasing block + traces of the 2026-07-27 shape-bench build; broken metric reported plain-saw BLEP -27.7 dB (leakage floor), midpoint sampling reported -149.5 dB and revealed fractional sync as a -62 dB hot spot that the broken metric had hidden. | falsifier: a residue metric that sums all non-signal bins is shown to agree with midpoint sampling on a case where the signal has many strong components — then leakage is not dominating and the simple sum is adequate. | supersedes: — (the general procedure is [[L0016]]; this is its spectral case)

[L0016] CALIBRATE THE DETECTOR BEFORE TRUSTING IT — run any new defect metric on a known-CLEAN and a known-BAD signal first; a metric that does not report ~zero on the clean case is measuring the signal, not the defect | canonical | added: 2026-07-28 | tags: parity-oracle, realtime-perf | lesson: THREE TIMES IN ONE SESSION I built a measurement instrument, got numbers that looked reasonable, and was measuring the wrong thing entirely. (1) An aliasing metric summed every non-harmonic FFT bin — it was accumulating Hann-window leakage from a dozen strong harmonics across thousands of bins, and reported a textbook-clean polyBLEP sawtooth at -27.7 dB instead of -149.5 dB. (2) A formant locator detrended the spectrum by +20log10(k) to cancel the saw's rolloff — that tilt over-weights the top octaves so hard it finds noise, and it reported 3190 Hz for an 800 Hz target. (3) A click detector thresholded |x[n]-x[n-1]| — on a 1200 Hz sine that is just the slope (~0.17/sample at 44.1 kHz), so it reported 2700 "jumps" in a perfectly clean signal, and I nearly chased a bug that did not exist. THE COMMON SHAPE: a threshold on the WRONG DERIVATIVE (or the wrong domain) measures the signal itself rather than the defect. WHY IT SURVIVES SCRUTINY: in all three cases the RANKING between configurations stayed roughly correct, so A/B comparisons still "worked" and nothing looked broken; only the absolute value was absurd. What saved me each time was noticing the absolute number was implausible against a reference I happened to know (a clean polyBLEP saw is ~-150 dB, not -27). That is luck, and it fails silently whenever I do NOT already know the right answer. THE RULE, which is cheap because both cases almost always already exist: before trusting a new detector, run it on (a) a signal known to be CLEAN — it MUST report ~zero, and if it does not, it is measuring the signal; and (b) a signal known to be DEFECTIVE — it MUST report large. Only then interpret real results. Corollary: plausible rankings are NOT validation. Domain-general — applies to any residue, noise floor, crosstalk, error rate, or A-minus-B metric where B is orders of magnitude larger than A. [[L0014]] is the spectral-specific technique that falls out of this (sample where the signal structurally ISN'T); this entry is the general procedure. | evidence: 2026-07-28 shape-lab session, all three instances measured and corrected: aliasing -27.7 -> -149.5 dB after inter-harmonic midpoint sampling (and it then revealed an 87 dB spread the broken metric had compressed to 15 dB, incl. the fractional-sync hot spot it hid entirely); formant locator replaced with a 1/3-octave envelope peak; click detector replaced with 2nd-difference outliers vs the robust local median, which then correctly read 0/s on integer sync and 300/s on ratio 4.37. | falsifier: a detector that passes the clean/defective calibration pair is later shown to be measuring the wrong quantity anyway — then calibration is insufficient and a stronger validation (e.g. an independently-derived reference implementation) is required. | supersedes: — (generalises [[L0014]], which stays as the spectral case)

[L0017] Detector calibration is per SIGNAL CLASS, not once per detector | tier: canonical | added: 2026-07-29 | tags: measurement, oracle, dsp, epistemics | lesson: L0016 says calibrate a new defect metric before trusting it. The unstated half: a calibration certifies the detector FOR THE SIGNAL CLASS IT WAS CALIBRATED ON. Carrying a validated detector to a different class silently re-opens the hole. Re-run the known-clean and known-bad cases every time the signal under test changes character (broadband -> harmonic, steady -> transient, mono -> stereo). | evidence: 2026-07-29 reverb stereo work. A sparse probe-frequency (Goertzel sweep) HF/LF metric was calibrated on the reverb tail and read exactly 0.00 dB on two independent mono configurations — genuinely valid there, because the tail is broadband. Reused unchanged on the DRY saw swarm it reported 4.62 dB of tilt at n=7 and -10.76 dB at n=8; the probe grid was landing on harmonics of one detuned voice and missing another's. Rebuilt as a Welch-averaged FFT integrating every bin per band (identical L/R -> 0.000 dB; known 6 dB HF lift -> +9.79 dB), the true worst was 0.65 dB across n=2..12. I had already written and measured a "fix" for the phantom defect before catching it. | RECURRED TWICE 2026-08-03 (promoted candidate->canonical on independent recurrence): building a click detector for the FX comb, I twice compared the wrong classes. First the floor was a DRY SINE (0.0078/sample slope) against a RINGING COMB, whose resonances legitimately exceed it — the bound was meaningless and the correct fix 'failed' it. Then two different NOTE PATTERNS, which differ in line count and entry time, so it measured their configurations rather than the click. Both were fixed by pairing WITHIN one run — worst jump near a note-on vs far from one, same signal, only position differs — which separated 1.01 (fixed) from 16.96 (planted bad). [L0018] A symmetric phase-warp axis is spectrally SIGN-BLIND | tier: candidate | added: 2026-07-29 | tags: dsp, spectra, design, measurement | lesson: For any waveshaping expressed as a monotone phase MAP, the map at +d and the map at -d are time reversals of each other, and a magnitude spectrum is invariant under time reversal — only the phase flips. [L0019] Humanised timing is CORRECTED error, not independent jitter | tier: canonical | added: 2026-07-29 | tags: timing, perception, humanize, midi, ensemble, doctrine, cross-project | lesson: Listeners judge ensemble togetherness from the SERIAL STRUCTURE of onset asynchrony, not from its variance. Model mutual error correction instead: each part nudges its offset toward the group's mean offset and then adds fresh motor noise, `off_i <- off_i - alpha*(off_i - mean_off) + noise_i`. The human-like lag-1 autocorrelation is then EMERGENT, not dialled in. Two design consequences worth more than the formula: (1) the musically meaningful control is alpha (the correction gain — how hard players pull toward each other, i.e. "tightness"), not the noise amount, which is why humanize knobs calibrated in milliseconds feel wrong at every setting; (2) alpha = 0 is an independent random walk that DRIFTS without bound, and alpha = 1 is rigid quantisation, so the usable musical range is strictly interior — a humanize feature with no correction term has no tightness axis at all, only a sloppiness axis. Applies to any onset-timing humanisation on MIDI or audio: DAW humanize, sequencer swing/feel, drum machines, sample libraries, unison/ensemble patches, multi-agent performance timing. | evidence: Wing, Endo, Bradbury & Vorberg (2014), string-quartet timing. Surfaced in HYPERSAW's 2026-07-28 orchestral-ensemble research pass (104 agents, 3-vote adversarial verification) and implemented as `EnsembleTiming` in docs/design/ensemble-lab.html. Independent corroboration of the mechanism from the other direction: a Kuramoto phase coupling K is formally the same error-correction gain, so a coupled-oscillator architecture generates this timing natively rather than needing a bolted-on humaniser — the strongest architecture/evidence match the project has found. Recorded as canonical on human review (2026-07-29) rather than on a second occurrence. | falsifier: a listening test in which matched-variance onset jitter with human-like lag-1 autocorrelation and with zero autocorrelation are indistinguishable; or a humanize implementation whose perceived musicality tracks jitter magnitude alone with serial structure held irrelevant. Note the claim is about PERCEIVED ensemble togetherness — it does not assert that variance is inaudible, only that structure dominates it.

[L0020] A BUILT ARTIFACT MUST DISPLAY ITS OWN PROVENANCE — "am I running what I just built?" is a question the artifact answers, not the human | tier: canonical | added: 2026-07-31 | tags: build, caching, provenance, debugging, doctrine, cross-project | lesson: Any artifact a human evaluates through a SEPARATE RUNTIME — a plugin loaded by a DAW, a page served to a browser, an app installed to a system path, a container pulled by an orchestrator, a model served behind an endpoint — sits behind at least one cache the human cannot see. [L0022] A rejected best-effort push must be RETRIED, never assumed delivered | tier: canonical | added: 2026-08-03 | tags: api, realtime, events, plugins, debugging, doctrine | lesson: When an API hands back a success flag (try_push, try_send, non-blocking write, ring-buffer enqueue), it is telling you the call CAN fail under load.
