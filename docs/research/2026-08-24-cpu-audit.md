# CPU audit — recommendations recorded and never taken

**Date** 2026-08-24 · **Roadmap** B41 step (1) · **Trigger** human:
*"Maybe you already recommended some things we never ended up doing."*

The answer is yes — six of them, and one is both the largest and the cheapest.
Every claim below was verified against the cited source, not taken on report.

## The standing measurement, for context

All figures below sit against the E-6 budget: **min-spec must hold under 50% of
one core** at 44.1 kHz / 128 samples (`ROADMAP.md:9`).

| case | measured | ×4 min-spec derate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 osc, parameter ceiling (32v × 16n = 512 osc) | 16.23% | **64.9% — over budget** |
| 2 osc, polyphony ceiling (16v × 16n) | 16.90% | **67.6% — over budget** |
| default patch (7v × 8n) | 1.60–1.81% | ~7% |

Source: `docs/research/2026-08-22-two-osc-cpu-measurement.md:71,99`. The ceiling
is already over budget **with zero filters, zero FX chain and one engine
family** — which is what makes the list below urgent rather than tidy.

---

## 1. ~~No denormal protection~~ — STRUCK 2026-08-25: the falsifier fired

**Measured, both architectures, and the finding's own falsifier condition was
met — so per its own terms it is struck, not softened.** `user_patch_bench`
with `HZ_FTZ=1` (FE_DFL_DISABLE_DENORMS_ENV on arm64, MXCSR FTZ|DAZ on SSE):

| | baseline | FTZ/DAZ | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| M3 (arm64), 8 s release tail | ~9.1% | ~9.2% | noise |
| EPYC 7763 (x86_64), 8 s release tail | ~21.6% | ~21.5% | noise |

The falsifier said "under ~2% difference → the finding is wrong". It is under
0.5%. The likely mechanism: this engine's decays never *linger* in denormal
range — voices retire at env < 1e-4, some 300 orders of magnitude above
subnormal territory, so the states the reasoning worried about are culled
before they get there. The reasoning was shape-correct and the shape does not
occur. FTZ at `process()` entry remains harmless cheap insurance if ever
wanted, but it is no longer *recommended work*, and B41 should not spend on it.

The original text follows for the record:

## ~~1. (original)~~ No denormal protection exists anywhere — and nobody has ever measured it

**Status: never proposed as work, never done. Probably the best unclaimed win.**

`grep -rniE 'denormal|FTZ|DAZ|flush.to.zero|_MM_SET|MXCSR|__fpcr' src/` returns
**zero hits** (verified with a control term to prove the search fires). The
concern was recorded once, in passing, about a feature that was never built —
routing feedback, `ROADMAP.md:3451-3466`: *"denormals in a decaying loop, which
is a real CPU cliff."* The feature stalled, so the concern was never generalised
to the code that already exists.

**Why this matters more than the note that raised it.** This synth is built out
of exponential decays: every voice envelope is a one-pole, release tails
asymptote toward zero and never arrive (that is the mechanism behind finding 2),
and the filter and notch cores carry decaying state. A one-pole approaching zero
is *precisely* the shape that lands in denormal range and stays there. On x86
that is a 10–100× per-operation penalty; the E-6 min-spec explicitly includes
"2018 Intel ultrabook / Win x64 AVX2" (`ROADMAP.md:9`), so the platform where
this bites hardest is one we have committed to supporting.

**Cost to fix:** setting FTZ/DAZ once at `process()` entry. **Cost to measure:**
one `user_patch_bench` run with and without. **Neither has been done.** This is
the only item on the list that is plausibly large, cheap, and free of any sound
ruling — flush-to-zero is inaudible by construction at −300 dB.

*Caveat, stated because it is not yet evidence:* the size is **unmeasured here**.
The reasoning above is sound and standard, but this repo's own history (finding
6, and ADR-091 A4's polyphony crash) shows the intuitive candidate is often not
the cost. Measure before believing the number.

## 2. Release tails burn full price for ~7 tau

**Status: OPEN since 2026-08-20, needs a sound ruling.**

`ROADMAP.md:278-290`, measured on the human's own patch via `user_patch_bench`:

> a 5 s release keeps every voice rendering for ~35 s … **Eight seconds after an
> 8-note release the CPU had not dropped at all.**

**This substantially answers the open question in B38.** I filed B38 saying "we
do not know what fraction of engine cost release tails are" and gated the work
on a `cpu_bench` cost-vs-threshold run. That gate was too strong: the measurement
showing tails cost *full price and do not decay* already exists. What is still
missing is only the shape of the curve — how much a *given* threshold buys — not
whether there is anything there. B38 is updated accordingly.

Three options were recorded, all of which change the sound or the knob's
meaning and so need a ruling rather than an optimisation commit: redefine the
release knob as time-to-−60 dB; add a faster-than-exponential floor near the
end; or leave the sound alone and make tail voices cheaper. **The human's B38
proposal is a fourth and better-shaped option** — expose the threshold, default
unchanged, and let the player make the trade.

## 3. controlTick's order-parameter trig at K = 0 — 15–20% of the render bill

**Status: OPEN since 2026-08-20, blocked on a viz ruling, never resolved.**

`ROADMAP.md:299-308`:

> ~15-20% of the remaining render bill is controlTick's order-parameter loops
> (sincos over every oscillator, twice, plus atan2) running at K = 0 — where the
> coupling force they feed is multiplied by exactly zero.

Three options were recorded (compute at viz rate on the GUI snapshot; gate and
accept a frozen ring at K = 0; leave it). **The ruling was never made, so the
15–20% is still being spent.** The entry's own warning stands: *"do not take it
as a stealth optimisation."*

### CORRECTED 2026-08-26 — the finding was right and its two load-bearing details were wrong

Read at the code rather than carried from the entry (`swarm_core.h:1564-1582`,
`:1636`, `:1734`), on the human's request to understand it before ratifying.
`controlTick` runs every `kTick = 16` samples and contains **two** loops:

| loop | computes | cost per tick per voice | audio consumer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `sx,sy` → `R`, `psi` | 2n sincos + `atan2` + `sqrt` | coupling force (`:1636`, ×`KsmS`, **zero at K=0**) **and `rtone` (`:1734`)** |
| 2 | `nx,ny` → `RN` | 2n sincos + `sqrt` | **none** |

**1. "Skipping is output-identical for audio" is FALSE when `rtone` ≠ 0.**
`R→Tone` (param 12) reads `s.R` at `:1734` to set a filter cutoff —
`fc = 16000·2^(-6·rtone·R)` — and that path is **not** gated by K. So the
proposed gate `K != 0 || absK != 0 || Kenv > eps` is incomplete: it must also
carry `|rtone| > eps`, or a K=0 patch with R→Tone engaged changes its sound.
`rtone` defaults to 0, so the default patch is safe — which is exactly why a
gate written against defaults would have shipped the bug.

**2. The RN loop has NO audio consumer at all — at any K.** `s.RN` is written
once (`:1582`), copied to the viz snapshot (`hypersaw_clap.cpp:1989`), drawn in
`gui.html`, and read by `trajectory_check`. It never reaches the DSP. So half
this cost is **unconditionally** non-audio, not merely wasted at K = 0, and
gating it needs no K condition and no rtone condition. Caveat: `trajectory_check`
asserts on RN through the viz snapshot, so moving it to viz rate changes *when*
it is sampled — an oracle question to settle, not a blocker.

**Net effect on the ruling:** the K = 0 half is a *narrower* win than recorded
(it needs the rtone term) and the RN half is a *wider* one (it applies always).

## 4. swarm_core calls libm raw; the fix is already proven elsewhere in this project

**Status: never proposed for swarm_core. Adjacent work shipped, and was not carried across.**

- `swarm_core.h` contains **63** raw `std::sin/cos/atan2/exp/pow` call sites and
  **no lookup table** (its three "table" matches are all comments — verified).
- `spectra_core.h` **does** have one: a 4096-point interpolated `SINE[4097]`
  built at construction (`spectra_core.h:90`, used at `:324`).
- Profile: **renderSeg 67%, libm transcendentals ~23%** (`DECISIONS.md:1618`).

Two techniques for removing exactly this cost have already been proven inside
this project and never applied to the founding engine: the SPECTRA sine LUT
above (measured 5.4× on the lab loop), and the **rotation recurrence** from
ADR-091 A3 (`DECISIONS.md:2653-2668`, 2026-08-23) — *"rotate a unit vector by w
per sample … verified equal to the closed form to 1e-14"* — which replaced
~77M libm calls/sec in the granular lab.

**This is a transfer failure, not a missing idea.** The knowledge exists in the
repo, was validated to 1e-14, and stopped at the module boundary. Any port here
is a parity question first (ε = 1e-6 RMS against the JS reference), which is
what makes it real work rather than a drop-in.

## 5. SPECTRA's adaptive oscillator budget — 76% → 36%, measured, never ported

**Status: measured in the JS lab 2026-08-04, never built in the core.**

`ROADMAP.md:7119-7124` recommends *"an oscillator budget that thins the CLOUD on
the highest partials first"*, with a measured worst case going **2016 → 636
oscillators, 76% → 36%**.

`spectra_core.h` carries only fixed static caps (`kPMax = 32, kMMax = 7`,
line 44) — `grep -ciE 'budget|thin|adaptive'` returns **0**. The companion
recommendation from the same entry (sine LUT + precomputed pan) **was** shipped,
so this is one half of a two-part proposal landing alone.

Lower urgency than the rest: it is gated behind B36's engine-roster ruling
(*"leaving spectra unreachable for now while we work out which engines make it
into the final version"*), so it should not be built before that is decided.

## 6. ~~No min-spec measurement~~ — RESOLVED 2026-08-25: the derate is ×2.2–2.5, not ×4

Measured on a real x86_64 (CI runner, AMD EPYC 7763, gcc 13 -O3):

| metric | M3 | EPYC 7763 | derate |
|---|---|---|---|
| cpu_bench default (7v×8n) | 1.60–1.81% | 4.04–4.07% | **×2.2–2.5** |
| user_patch_bench tail | ~9.1% | ~21.6% | **×2.36** |

At ×2.5 the parameter-ceiling case (16.23% on M3) derates to **~41% — inside
the 50% E-6 budget**, where the borrowed ×4 said 64.9% and "over". B41's and
B42's urgency framing soften accordingly. Caveats, stated so this number is
not over-trusted the way ×4 was: one machine; a Zen 3 server core is not a
2018 ultrabook (the E-6 floor) — it is likely FASTER per-core, so ×2.2–2.5 is
a *lower bound* on the true min-spec derate; and the ratio was measured at the
default patch and the tail shape, not at the ceiling case itself. The
standing instrument: `.github/workflows/cpu-derate.yml`, one click.

The original text follows for the record:

## ~~6. (original)~~ No min-spec hardware measurement — every number here is extrapolated

**Status: recommended three times (2026-08-06, 2026-08-22, 2026-08-24), never done.**

`docs/research/2026-08-22-two-osc-cpu-measurement.md:179`: *"no min-spec hardware
was available for this measurement … that gap is reported, not closed."*

**Every CPU figure in this repo — including all of the above — is measured on an
Apple M3 and multiplied by a borrowed ×4 derate.** The ×4 is a heuristic, not a
measurement. The recommendation to run `cpu_bench` on CI runners
(`windows-latest`/`ubuntu-latest`) as a real x86 proxy costs almost nothing and
has never been actioned, and `dist/cpu_bench` already exists as a universal
signed binary for exactly this.

**This gates the interpretation of everything else on this page.** If the true
derate is ×2, nothing is over budget; if it is ×6, the default patch is closer
to the edge than anyone has assumed. We are making architectural decisions
against a number nobody has checked.

## Suggested order

1. **Measure the derate** (finding 6) — cheapest, and it rescales every other
   number on this page. Until it is done we are optimising against a guess.
2. **Measure denormals** (finding 1) — cheap, no ruling needed, plausibly large.
3. **Rule on the K = 0 trig** (finding 3) — the win is already quantified at
   15–20%; only a viz decision is missing.
4. **Rule on release tails** (finding 2 / B38) — quantified as "does not decay
   at all"; the human's threshold proposal is the best-shaped option.
5. **Port the transcendental work** (finding 4) — real work, parity-gated.
6. **SPECTRA thinning** (finding 5) — hold for the B36 roster ruling.

## Falsifier

If a `user_patch_bench` A/B with FTZ/DAZ enabled shows under ~2% difference,
finding 1 is wrong and should be struck rather than left as a standing
suggestion. Likewise, if the CI-runner derate measures at or under ×2, the
"already over budget" framing in B41 is wrong and the ceiling cases are fine.
