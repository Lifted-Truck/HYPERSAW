fx morph-law lab rev 1 · 2026-08-26 · B50

Corner A routes source → DRIVE → COMB → out. Corner B routes the same two modules the other way: source → COMB → DRIVE → out. Both corners are the same modules with the same settings — only the order differs. The question this lab exists to settle is what should happen between them, and it is a question you answer by ear, not by argument. Rule on the law, not on the preset: try it on the drone and on the pluck, because sustained and transient material disagree about this.

Transport

Everything is generated here — a seeded 7-voice detuned saw bank, no samples, no network. Start audio first; browsers will not open an AudioContext without a click.

The control — do the two orders even differ?

Check this first. If A and B sound the same on your material, the whole question is moot and any law is fine — turn DRIVE and COMB up until the order is obviously audible before judging anything below. Distorting a resonant comb and combing a distorted signal are different operations; if you cannot hear that, the test is not loaded.

Modules

Deliberately strong defaults: a subtle drive and a shy comb make every law sound identical, which would be a false negative rather than a result.

Output

—

Measured before you got here, so you are not ruling blind. With these defaults, BLEND's midpoint runs +9.0 dB louder than corner A and clips (peak 1.17) — both paths sum and the live cycle adds loop gain. ARGMAX's midpoint is just corner B's level; DIP's is silence, exactly 0. So BLEND is not merely a taste question: as written it needs gain compensation before it could ship at all. Judge whether it is worth compensating.

The scope is orientation, not evidence. A meter cannot tell you whether the middle of the sweep is musical; only listening can. Judge with your ears and use the trace only to confirm something is actually reaching the output.
