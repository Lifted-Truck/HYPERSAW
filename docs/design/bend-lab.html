SWARM✱ bend lab

Bench for pitch-bend inertia (human request 2026-08-03) — pitch does not arrive where you point it, it travels there. Four models, because "inertia" is four different physical claims and they do not sound alike: a lag is proportional (every move takes the same time regardless of size), a rate limit is constant-velocity (big moves take longer, small ones are instant), and only the mass-spring is inertia in the literal sense — it can overshoot and ring, because a mass in motion does not stop when the force does. The bench measures each: step response with lag / overshoot / settle / reversals, plus a vibrato test, because the price of inertia is that it eats fast wheel vibrato and that cost should be a number, not a surprise. Keys A..;, Z/X octave, space drone, drag the wheel, or fire a gesture (identical input = a fair A/B, which a hand-dragged wheel can never give you).

Inertia model

Only the knobs your model reads are live. return × scales the time constant when the bend is heading back toward zero — a real spring returns faster than you can push, so <1 gives the snap-back; it is bend-lane only (a note has no "home"). damping ζ: 1 = critical (fastest arrival with no overshoot), <1 rings, >1 goes sluggish. Note that spring on note pitch is the interesting one — it puts a pitch blip on every note onset, which is what a real resonator does when it is struck.

Wheel & gestures

tap is the diagnostic one: an instant there-and-back that no hand can perform. A lag just rounds it off; a spring keeps moving after your hand has stopped, which is the whole point of the model. dive is −12 st and back — the case where a rate limit and a lag part company completely.

Swarm

No coupling in this bench on purpose: K does not touch the pitch lane, and leaving it out means nothing else can be blamed for what you hear. Detune and drift stay because bend on a swarm is the case that matters — a moving pitch drags 7 slightly-different pitches with it.
