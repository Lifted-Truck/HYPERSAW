SWARM✱ routing lab — topology bench

B23. The question is not which effects to have — it is which sources reach which slots, in what order, and who pays for it. Three candidate topologies, the same four-slot rack (ADR-054 is already a grid, not a fixed chain), the same two sources. Switch schemes while it plays: the audio is the argument, and the cost table is the other half of it, because CLAP param ids are append-only — a topology you cannot afford in ids is not a topology you can ship.

Trim before you judge. The three schemes are NOT the same loudness at equal settings — measured at one rack config: A 0.398, B 0.792, C 0.847 RMS. A parallel sum of four slots, two private serial chains, and a DAG with a couple of terminals simply deliver different amounts of signal, and in an A/B the loud one always sounds better. The trim is remembered PER SCHEME, and the live RMS is there so matching is a number rather than a hunch.
Sources are deliberately plain saws, not swarms: the subject is the graph, and a swarm's own stereo image would mask what the routing is doing.

What each scheme costs

Params counted for 2 oscillators × 4 slots, and again at 4 × 8 — the second column is the one that decides, because a topology is chosen once and lived with. FX state is per-instance DSP memory: a scheme that gives each oscillator private slots pays for the delay lines twice.
