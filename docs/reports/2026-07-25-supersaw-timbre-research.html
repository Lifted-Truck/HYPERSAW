1 · The origin story just changed

The JP-8000 SuperSaw has been read off the silicon (39C3, Dec 2025 — automated silicon RE of the four custom Roland/Toshiba TC170C140 DSPs, plus a bit-accurate JIT emulator, JE-8086). The result is deflationary: seven naive (non-bandlimited) sawtooths, summed, through a high-pass filter, with aliasing dodged by running at an 88.2 kHz internal rate. No phase tricks. No chorus. No hidden modulation.

Why this matters to us: it removes a temptation. There is no lost Roland secret to recover — so every metallic/glassy reach has to come from HYPERSAW's own additions. It also retires a myth we could otherwise have chased.

Caveats recorded: the emulator ships without firmware (needs a user ROM dump — weak as a CI oracle, legally grey); bit-accuracy is developer-asserted with no published third-party hardware null test; one popular retelling (CDM) misidentifies the chip as a Motorola 56300, which is the Virus chip, not this one.

Szabo's 2010 thesis survives as the detune-law reference, even though superseded as an architecture oracle: the detune curve is measurably non-linear and non-proportional, fitted with an 11th-degree polynomial, with an asymmetric, unequally-spaced set of side-oscillator ratios and a centre oscillator that detune does not move at all. Most of the knob's travel produces very small offsets. we already implement this — the JP-8000 distribution

The single most useful finding for this project. Peer-reviewed synthesis research states outright that static spectral richness is not sufficient for a convincing supersaw — the missing ingredient is time-varying timbral variation from the detuned bank. That is independent academic support for HYPERSAW's entire premise: the instrument's identity is the animation, not the stacked spectrum. Worth citing in SPEC.

2 · Two mechanisms that land inside the saw mandate

Hard sync, as a pure phase operation category (ii) — add a parameter

The classic route to aggressive/metallic timbre does not require a second oscillator. It reduces to a phaseshaping composition on a single modulo-1 phase counter:

y(n) = 2·[a₁·x(n) mod 1] − 1, with a₁ = f_slave / f_master

Our swarm already maintains exactly that per-voice normalised modulo-1 phase, and g_b in the source yields a hard-synced sawtooth slave specifically — the wanted case under the saw mandate. Applying it to a coupling-perturbed phase is well defined, which means sync ratio becomes a per-voice swarm parameter, and the Kuramoto coupling can modulate it.

Cost: the trivial form is alias-prone (the modulo makes discontinuities); the source's own remedy is polyBLEP — which we already have in the saw oscillator, so budget a 2-sample correction per modulo transition.

SMC 2010 §3.3 (Kleimola/Lazzarini/Välimäki/Timoney), verified verbatim from PDF · extended by DAFx-11 VPS and Lazzarini/Timoney DAFx-12 · zenodo.org/records/849811

Formants without a filter category (ii) — add a parameter

Vowel-like and "talking" character — the spectral signature behind both the glassy hyperpop palette and the growl direction — is obtainable inside the oscillator, with no filter in the signal path. Three independently published mechanisms:

  • Variable-slope phaseshaping — a sinusoidal shaper on a variable-slope triangular phase produces a prominent formant region positioned by a₁; outside that region every fourth harmonic is missing.
  • Vector Phaseshaping (VPS) — formant centre at f_f/f₀ = 2v − 1, so formants land on exact harmonics at v = 1.5, 2, 2.5…
  • Phase-synchronous ModFM — formant placed at an arbitrary f_f by cross-fading two carriers on adjacent harmonics of f_m, crossfade coefficient = the fractional part of the ratio.

Two caveats that shape the design, both from the papers themselves: (1) aliasing appears where 2v−1 is non-integral, and the published fix crossfades two VPS oscillators — so "single oscillator" holds for a static formant, not for an alias-free glide; (2) bandwidth is coupled to position — offsetting the shaper spreads the formant and raises brightness and aliasing together, unlike a resonant filter's independent fc/Q. A formant knob here will not behave like a filter, and shouldn't be labelled as though it does.

4 · On a third engine

The case is not made. The best-attested candidate premise is the Janus oscillator network: a ring where each node holds two internally coupled phase oscillators with natural frequencies of equal magnitude and opposite sign (internal coupling β, external nearest-neighbour σ). It satisfies the mandate structurally — Kuramoto is a reduction of it, so it is a minimal extension rather than a new bag of effects.

But its timbral payoff is unevidenced — the specific claim that it yields a wider set of dynamical regimes was refuted under verification. So it is an interesting physical premise with no demonstrated sonic reason to build it yet.

Recommendation: exhaust category (ii) first. Hard sync and oscillator-internal formants both land in the existing saw swarm, and between them they target the metallic direction directly. If those are built and the glassy/growl space is still out of reach, that failure becomes the actual evidence for a third engine — which is a much better basis than starting one now.

5 · Prior art — the territory is emptier than expected

The nearest published coupled-oscillator audio synthesis work, the Kuroscillator, is a bank of phase-coupled sine waves with network frequencies capped at 0.25–30 Hz. It contains no sawtooth, no supersaw, and no metallic or growl mechanism. It is prior art for coupled-oscillator additive/rhythmic synthesis — not for a coupled detuned-saw swarm. Its one transferable contribution is the mean-field O(N) reduction, which carries a documented equation typo (already known to us).

No competitive-landscape claim about the commercial synths survived verification, so treat that space as unsurveyed rather than clear.

6 · The one verified artist-process datum

For the hyperpop direction, exactly one first-person statement survived: SOPHIE's — everything except vocals was synthesized from raw waveforms, samples explicitly rejected, with "metal" named as a synthesized target on an Elektron Monomachine. So the metallic palette is documented as reachable from synthesis primitives, not derived from sampled metal recordings. Everything else in the AG Cook / PC Music / Danny L Harle discourse that the swarm surfaced was folklore and did not survive verification — which is itself worth knowing before anyone chases a technique on the strength of a forum post.
