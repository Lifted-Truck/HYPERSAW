Glide travel laws — the roundup

Fold ruling 2026-08-06: laws 1–4 ship; law 5 does not. Scale quantise ships as a modifier on all four rather than as a sixth law — see the section at the end.
Every law measured from bend-lab.html's own Inertia class, evaluated headlessly — not reimplemented, so this table describes the synth you actually auditioned. Step: a ±2 semitone jump (bendRange is in semitones) held 600 ms then released, at the 2756 Hz control rate. Overshoot is reported in cents. lag 50 = time to cover half the distance · overshoot = how far past the target it goes · settle = last moment outside ±5¢ ("never" = asymptotic, it is still moving) · reversals = error sign flips, i.e. ringing · vibrato kept = fraction of a 5 Hz wheel wobble that survives, the price of inertia.

1 · constant time

Same duration near or far — velocity is set by the distance. What most synths mean by "portamento time".
lag 50 60 ms  ·  overshoot none  ·  settle 117 ms  ·  reversals 0  ·  vibrato kept 33%

2 · constant rate

Fixed cents/second, so distance sets the duration. A wide leap genuinely takes longer.
lag 50 41 ms  ·  overshoot none  ·  settle 81 ms  ·  reversals 0  ·  vibrato kept 93%

3 · lag

One-pole, asymptotic. Never technically arrives, so it has no glide TIME at all — only a time constant.
lag 50 41 ms  ·  overshoot none  ·  settle 221 ms  ·  reversals 0  ·  vibrato kept 47%

4 · spring

True inertia: a mass that overshoots and rings, because motion does not stop when the force does.
lag 50 54 ms  ·  overshoot 18.8¢  ·  settle 230 ms  ·  reversals 1  ·  vibrato kept 63%

5 · lag → const rate — NOT SHIPPING

Series: a soft departure into a fixed-rate traverse. NOT SHIPPING (human ruling 2026-08-06) — it is the closest pair to law 3 and adds a control without adding a behaviour.
lag 50 41 ms  ·  overshoot none  ·  settle 221 ms  ·  reversals 0  ·  vibrato kept 47%

How distinct are they, really?

Peak divergence between each pair of laws over the same step, in cents. This is the check the metrics table cannot do for you: two laws can round to identical lag/settle/vibrato figures and still be different curves — or be near-duplicates wearing different names.
pairpeak divergence
3 lag  vs  5 lag → const rate 8.70¢
1 constant time  vs  4 spring 25.29¢

1 constant time  vs  3 lag 30.69¢
Law 3 (lag) and law 5 (lag → constant rate) are the closest pair. Their lag 50, settle, reversals and vibrato figures are identical to the printed precision, and the curves still diverge by up to 8.7¢ mid-flight — audible, but small. At τ 60 ms the lag stage does most of the work before the rate limiter binds. Whether that earns a separate law, or whether law 5 wants a shorter default τ to show its character, is a judgement call for the fold decision (A1).

Scale quantise — a modifier, not a sixth law

Applied to the emitted pitch while the law's dynamics run untouched underneath, so it composes with all four: spring + quantise is an overshooting autotune wobble, constant-rate + quantise a stepped portamento. As a sixth law it would have been one behaviour; as a modifier it is four.

Hysteresis fixes one specific artefact, and it is worth knowing which. A step change is the output jumping to a new quantised degree; too many means chatter.

Hysteresis rescues the parked-on-a-boundary case (15→3), stops helping once the ringing dwarfs the window (a damping problem, not a quantiser one), and correctly does nothing to a wide deliberate vibrato — that motion is supposed to step. The first draft of this note claimed hysteresis was simply "not optional"; the measurement disagreed and the claim was narrowed.
