2026-07-28 · 104-agent swarm, 5 angles → source fetch → 3-vote adversarial verification. 11 findings survived. Question: what does a real section have that a detuned oscillator bank lacks — and which of it is reachable here?
The headline validates the architecture, specifically and unexpectedly. Listeners judge ensemble "togetherness" not from the variance of note-onset asynchrony but from its serial micro-structure — the lag-1 autocorrelation produced by players mutually correcting each other's timing errors. A Kuramoto coupling K is formally that error-correction gain. So the right way to generate ensemble timing is the coupled dynamics we already have, at low-to-moderate K — and independent per-note random jitter, which is what every conventional "humanize" control does, is the wrong mechanism.

1 · Static detune should be far TIGHTER than a supersaw

Measured inter-source pitch scatter in real unison ensembles is 13–30 cents SD — not the tens of cents a supersaw typically uses. Larger instantaneous figures (39–55 cents) are inflated by vibrato, flutter and note-transition spikes (~150–200 cents decaying over ~0.3 s), not by static per-voice offset.

More pointed: in controlled tests on synthesized five-voice sections, expert listeners set the maximum tolerable static scatter at ~14 cents SD, but when asked for preference chose 0 or 5 cents. Critically the stimuli already carried per-voice vibrato and flutter — so this bounds static mean-F0 offset only, and is not evidence that listeners want a phase-locked, variation-free unison. The variation should be in time, not in static tuning.

Lottermoser & Meyer 1960 via Ternström; Jers & Ternström 2005 (16-singer choir, per-singer nose mics, <1 cent resolution); Ternström & Sundberg 1988 (~13 cents, six basses); Cuesta & Gómez 2019 (arXiv:1904.05086, 20–30 cents, methodologically independent).

2 · The biggest verified gap: onset scatter

Between-player onset asynchrony in professional ensembles has an SD in the tens of milliseconds — 49 ms (string trio, 79 bpm), 32 ms (wind trio, 88 bpm), 24–28 ms (two string quartets, 157 bpm); full range 24–73 ms, strongly tempo-dependent. The threshold for discriminating a perfectly-synchronous rendering from a scattered one is ~8 ms SD — roughly 3–5× below what real ensembles produce.

Our swarm currently has exactly zero onset scatter. Every voice of a note starts on the same sample. By this evidence that is reliably distinguishable from an ensemble.

3 · …but generate it with coupling, not jitter

Wing et al. 2014, verbatim: "people are sensitive to both the degree of variance and the micro-structure of the time-series of the asynchronies caused by differences in correction gain when judging lack of togetherness." Detection threshold in asynchrony-variance terms fell from 64.3 ms² to 18.2 ms² when serial structure differed; lag-1 autocorrelation 0.84 vs 0.39 (p<0.0005). Less variance was needed for detection when the structure changed.

Their stimuli were generated by a linear phase-correction model — i.e. mutual error correction, which is what Kuramoto coupling is. This is the strongest architecture/evidence match in the report.

Held at medium confidence for methodological reasons, not dissent: gain and asynchrony variance are not orthogonal in the Vorberg/Wing model, n=9 untrained listeners. Note their caveat that a near-zero-variability reference "might be considered unrealistic for musical contexts" does NOT bite here — for a synth, zero variability is exactly what we have.

4 · Attack is chaotic, and that is physical

Bowed-string attacks occupy a narrow wedge in the bow-force/bow-acceleration plane (Guettler), with categorically different failure modes on each side: over-force gives raucous/scratchy transients, under-force gives loose multiple-slipping ones. And decisively: machine-bowed repeats under nominally identical force and acceleration produce different transients — framed in the source as sensitive dependence on initial conditions.

So per-note attack-character randomization along a bidirectional scratchy ↔ loose axis is physically grounded, not cosmetic — and it is a different thing from attack-time jitter.

5 · Two dimensions orthogonal to pitch

Spectral smear. Dispersion of formants 3–5 from vocal-tract-length differences was manipulated as an independent dimension from detune. That is a per-voice spectral-position offset — and our windowed-carrier VOSIM/FOF formant axis with absolute-Hz lock is its faithful analogue. Per-voice LP/HP tone tilt is a cruder proxy, not an equivalent.

Vibrato is not reliably uncorrelated. In a measured 16-singer choir most singers showed partial vibrato synchronisation, attributed to shared note onsets acting as a common phase-resetting event. This contradicts the standard "uncorrelated per-source modulation" assumption behind chorus models — and note our note-on phase scatter already is a phase-reset mechanism. (Preliminary; choir, not strings.)

7 · What I'd do next

  • Onset scatter, generated by the coupling. The largest verified gap, and the one the architecture is uniquely suited to fill correctly rather than approximately.
  • Tighten the detune defaults toward 5–15 cents SD gaussian and let time-domain variation carry the ensemble character — the reverse of the usual supersaw instinct.
  • Per-voice formant offset — the windowed-carrier axis exists; making it per-voice is the "spectral smear" dimension.
  • A narrow second pass on orchestral space, since that angle returned nothing and it is where the convolution question lives.
