# Trace — the morph-law lab, and what it measured before anyone listened

**Trigger** human 2026-08-26: *"maybe we should run a lab demonstrating the two
ways to approach the mentioned collisions so I can rule on what sounds best."*

**What changed.** `docs/design/fx-morph-law-lab.html` (new lab),
`docs/design/index.html` (regenerated by `gen_lab_index.py`), ROADMAP B50
updated with the measurement. No engine code.

**Design decision worth recording.** The lab shares ONE Drive and ONE Comb
through a real 3×3 crosspoint rather than crossfading two private chains.
Those two constructions sound different, and only the shared one is what "set
modules + routing matrix" means — so only the shared one is the thing being
ruled on. That fidelity has a payoff: at BLEND's midpoint the graph genuinely
contains `drive→comb→drive`, Web Audio therefore requires a delay in the loop,
and its minimum is one render quantum (2.9 ms at 44.1 kHz) — which is exactly
the block-rate feedback latency B50 §2 warns about, made physical instead of
argued.

**Measured, driving the real transport (three laws × three positions):**

| law | corner A | midpoint | corner B | mid vs A | mid peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BLEND | 0.227 | **0.637** | 0.150 | **+9.0 dB** | **1.166 — clips** |
| ARGMAX | 0.252 | 0.130 | 0.135 | −5.7 dB | 0.167 |
| DIP | 0.219 | 0.000 | 0.142 | −∞ | 0.000 |

**This changes the shape of the ruling.** BLEND was framed as a taste question
("is parallel-in-the-middle musical?"). It is also a level failure: the
midpoint is 9 dB hotter than either endpoint and clips, because both paths sum
*and* the live cycle adds loop gain. So the real question is whether it is
worth gain-compensating, not whether it is pretty.

**The lab carries its own must-fire control.** Its first panel is an A/B of the
two endpoints, with the instruction to turn the modules up until the difference
is obvious before judging anything else — because if the two orders sound alike
on the chosen material, every law sounds alike and the test proves nothing.
Verified: 9.13 dB rms spectral distance between corners A and B at the defaults.

**Verification honesty.** The load gate (`lab_load_check`) only proves the
scripts execute; it cannot see whether an audio graph produces sound. So the
transport was driven in a browser and the analyser read directly — legitimate
here because the graph is native nodes with no worklet, so a non-zero reading
is real evidence signal exists. It is *not* evidence the result sounds right,
and the lab says so where the scope is drawn. **The ruling is the human's ear;
these numbers only make sure they are not ruling blind.**

**Verify.** `./verify fast` exit 0; `lab_load_check` GREEN. No engine code
touched, parity untouched by construction.
